How this works

The simulation

  • “Monthly” means every 30 days, not the same date each calendar month — about 12.2 buys a year, and the date drifts earlier over time. Weekly is 7 days, bi-weekly is 14.
  • Each purchase fills at that UTC day’s closing price — the most recent close on or before the date, never a later one. Intraday highs and lows are ignored.
  • No fees, spreads, or taxes. A platform charging ~1% a buy would leave you ~1% less Bitcoin every time, so every figure here is an optimistic upper bound. Nothing is ever sold — profit is unrealised.
  • Buying continues through today, so an earlier start date also means more total dollars invested. Compare runs on ROI, not on profit.

Reading the results

  • Average Cost per BTC lands below the average price you actually paid — a fixed dollar amount buys more Bitcoin when the price is low. That gap is dollar-cost averaging doing its job.
  • Return on Investment is the total across the whole duration, not a yearly rate. Don’t divide it by the number of years.
  • Portfolio Value prices your whole stack at the most recent daily close. In the history table, Value on Date prices it at that row’s date instead — which is why the last row and the summary differ.
